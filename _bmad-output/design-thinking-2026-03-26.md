## 🎯 Design Challenge

**Syntonie ASBL Website: Building Institutional Credibility & Enabling Governance Shelter**

### Challenge Statement

Syntonie ASBL needs a professional, authentic website that simultaneously:

1. **Conveys institutional credibility** to Erasmus+ grant assessors and municipal/institutional partners (demonstrating organizational soundness, governance maturity, evidence of impact)
2. **Invites secondary stakeholders** — project leaders from OpenFab/Politype — to consider Syntonie as a governance shelter for their own ventures
3. **Clearly articulates** vision, mission, values, and target audiences without contradicting the trinity-charter positioning
4. **Provides evidence of institutional activity** (flagship projects: Invisible Play, Allo-IA) to establish track record
5. **Clarifies contribution pathways** (membership, registration, pricing) while maintaining an authentic, non-corporate tone

### Target Audiences

| Audience | Needs | Urgency |
|----------|-------|---------|
| **Erasmus+ Assessors** | Institutional credibility, governance clarity, track record of impact | 🔴 URGENT (KA210 applications) |
| **Municipal/Institutional Partners** | Vision alignment, partnership pathways, evidence of outcomes | 🟠 High |
| **Project Leaders (OpenFab/Politype)** | Understanding governance shelter model, how to access, examples of enabled projects | 🟡 Medium |

### Constraints & Context

- **Timeline**: Urgent (Erasmus+ applications pending)
- **Tone**: Professional + authentic (associative, not corporate)
- **Differentiation**: Must isolate Syntonie's concerns (governance, legal, vision) while showing bridges to OpenFab/Politype
- **Flagship Context**: Two validated Erasmus+ projects provide proof of concept:
  - **Invisible Play**: Neuro-inclusive urban environments, youth engagement, game-based + citizen sensing
  - **Allo-IA**: AI-powered documentation for makerspaces, universal design, neurodivergent inclusion

## 👥 EMPATHIZE: Understanding Users

### User Insights

#### Segment A — Erasmus+ National Agency Assessors (LEBIJ type)

- They evaluate projects against program criteria (YOUTH, ADU, STEM alignment), not against familiarity with the innovation itself. They may never have visited a fablab.
- Their question is structural: *"Does this lead organization have the capacity, legitimacy, and track record to deliver this project?"* Not: *"Is this cool?"*
- The connection between Syntonie and the funded project (e.g., Invisible Play) must be instantly legible: they need to trace a line from **association purpose → project methodology → target beneficiaries → track record**
- Key validation triggers: organizational age, governance structure, previous EU-funded or youth-facing activities, named beneficiary groups (neurodiverse youth = vulnerable public = YOUTH program fit)
- They're working from a checklist, not browsing for inspiration. Speed of comprehension matters.

#### Segment B — Commune d'Ixelles (Municipal Partners)

- Existing relationship — not a cold audience. They need *justification content*, not persuasion.
- They evaluate Syntonie against their own political mandate: social cohesion, digital inclusion, ecological transition. Syntonie has already mapped these explicitly (3 strategic objectives in grant application).
- They need to see **territorial anchoring** (Ixelles, local actors, local impact) alongside **broader European reach** (to justify support for EU projects emerging from the local space)
- The municipality validates: "Are we funding something that serves our citizens and supports our policy agenda?"
- Quote value: they reuse Syntonie's own language in their reports. Clear mission statements = easy quote extraction.

#### Segment C — Project Leaders (via OpenFab/Politype)

- They don't arrive at Syntonie through Syntonie — they arrive through projects, workshops, human encounters at OpenFab
- The insight is sequential: **person → project → community → association**. Syntonie is revealed, not marketed.
- Their key realization is: "I have a project idea but I can't apply to this open call alone" → suddenly the association structure has value
- They value independence above all. They won't join if it feels like absorption. The pitch is **"you keep creative control, we provide the legal container"**
- Proof points that matter: previous sheltered projects with named outcomes (Speculoos, Fabulous, Fab-Ecole, Fablab Mobile)
- Emotional driver: belonging to an ecosystem that aligns with their values, not just a bureaucratic necessity

### Key Observations

1. **Three completely different jobs, one website** — The assessor wants instant credibility scanning; the municipality wants territorial impact evidence; the project leader wants to understand what becomes *possible*. These jobs almost never share the same navigation path.

2. **Syntonie is invisible by design** — It deliberately doesn't operate day-to-day. This is a strategic strength (governance purity) but a website liability (nothing visibly "happening" at Syntonie itself). The site must make *what it enables* visible, through OpenFab and Politype activity.

3. **Track record is the product** — For both assessors and municipalities, past activity is the main offer. Not vision (they can't evaluate that), not values (hard to verify) — but evidence: named projects, dated activities, measurable outcomes.

4. **The shelter narrative is not on the website yet** — Project leaders discover this organically through human relationships. There's an opportunity to make "use Syntonie as your legal shelter" a first-class, explicit offering — with clear process, conditions, and examples.

5. **Neurodiversity is a thread, not a feature** — It appears across Invisible Play, Allo-IA, OpenFab's community approach, and Syntonie's accessibility values. This isn't a niche specialty — it's a through-line that builds coherence and differentiation.

6. **Language gap** — Ixelles grant content is in French (institutional register). Erasmus+ content operates in English. The website must navigate this bilingual expectation without diluting either register.

## 🎨 DEFINE: Frame the Problem

### Point of View Statement

#### POV A — Erasmus+ Assessor (LEBIJ type)
**"A grant assessor needs to instantly verify organizational legitimacy and project delivery capacity because they evaluate compliance against program criteria — not innovation quality — and vague or visionary language neither satisfies their checklist nor builds the trust needed to approve public funds."**

Evidence anchor: Syntonie has been continuously operational since 3 April 2012 (13 years), has a fully active CA, renewed statutes (2019), 249+ cumulative members, and two EU-connected activities (Vulca network, JRC European Commission residency). All of this is verifiable and citable — but currently not surfaced as a scannable credential layer.

#### POV B — Municipal Partner (Commune d'Ixelles)
**"A municipal partner needs quotable, territorially-anchored impact language because they must justify continued partnership upward to their own political hierarchy — and Syntonie's mission statements, if well-crafted, become their own reporting vocabulary."**

Evidence anchor: Syntonie has operated from Ixelles-provided space since November 2019, has co-delivered youth workshops (Matongray / Let's Play, SALANBAYOU board game with AMO Dynamo + Atout Projet), and has been explicitly aligned with Ixelles' three strategic mandates: social cohesion (youth + community), digital inclusion (fablab + Allo-IA), ecological transition (repair + open tools). The municipality needs to see this reflected back in clear, reusable language.

#### POV C — Project Leader (from OpenFab/Politype ecosystem)
**"A project leader needs to understand concretely what 'Syntonie as governance shelter' enables — legally, practically, in terms of creative autonomy — because the fear of losing project control is their primary hesitation, and that fear can only be neutralized by named precedents and explicit process clarity."**

Evidence anchor: Syntonie has sheltered at least 4 named projects with documented outcomes: Speculoos (production equipment, Kesho Kongo DRC), Fabulous (16-maker cooperative, Fabulous2.0 museum models), Cabinet de l'Étrange (escape room hybrid, ILES + Seed & Bloom), Enigmaker (Maker Faire Brussels 2023, Vulca Maker Tour Europe). The shelter model works — it just isn't legible yet as an explicit offering.

### Key Insights

**1. The credibility gap is structural, not cosmetic.**
Syntonie has 13 years of evidence. The problem isn't the track record — it's the lack of a surface layer that makes this evidence immediately legible to people with checklists. The website needs a *credential layer*: scannable, structured, instantly cross-referenceable with grant program criteria. This is not about making the site look more professional. It's about organizing existing evidence for the right cognitive workflow.

**2. Syntonie's invisibility is its strongest differentiator — if framed correctly.**
The fact that Syntonie doesn't operate day-to-day is unusual in the nonprofit landscape. Most ASBLs are defined by their activities. Syntonie is defined by what it *makes possible*. This is a philosophical asset but a communication liability. The reframe: "We don't do — we enable. Here's what got enabled." Evidence: 4+ named incubated projects, European residencies, 249+ members choosing to sustain this structure.

**3. Three audiences, three completely different information jobs.**
Assessors want a compliance scan. Municipal partners want quotable territorial impact. Project leaders want permission to imagine their project inside Syntonie. These three jobs almost never share the same content or navigation path. Any attempt to serve all three from one undifferentiated homepage will underserve all three. The site needs explicit routing: "Are you evaluating us? Here. Are you a partner? Here. Do you have a project? Here."

**4. The shelter model is an untapped acquisition channel.**
Currently, project leaders discover Syntonie's shelter function through human relationships — serendipitously. There's no way to "discover" this via the website. Making it explicit (named process, named precedents, named benefits, named boundaries) turns a word-of-mouth function into a structured offering. This has strategic value beyond the current project leader segment.

**5. The neurodiversity thread is an organizational coherence signal.**
Neurodiversity appears across Invisible Play (neuro-inclusive urban environments), Allo-IA (AI documentation for neurodivergent inclusion), OpenFab's accessibility practice, and Syntonie's own governance values. For Erasmus+ YOUTH program alignment, this is not a niche specialty — it's a documented, cross-cutting commitment that signals organizational coherence and aligns with European vulnerable publics criteria.

**6. Language is a dual-register problem, not a translation problem.**
The institutional French register (for Ixelles, for LEBIJ) and the international English register (for Erasmus+ European partners, for global fablab network) are not the same content in two languages — they're different rhetorical registers for different institutional contexts. The site architecture must allow both to coexist without either feeling like a translation of the other.

### Advanced Elicitation — Key Reframes

**Critical reframe (from pressure-testing):**
- LEBIJ/municipality are **passive validators**, not primary audience. They need to find a credible, existing website — not a site designed for them.
- The **primary audience is the undiagnosed neurodivergent maker/builder** — people like Nicolas: HP + ADHD tendencies, top values: self-direction, stimulation, security, benevolence, universality. Silent majority. Looking for a place built by people like them.
- No cold forms. No phone number. No chatbot popup. No list of questions.
- Contact/project-sheltering is **human-first**: visit, meet, confirm fit. The website bridges "stranger with a question" → "person who shows up at OpenFab."
- Hugo static site — client-side JS + small serverless proxy (Cloudflare Worker / Netlify Function) for API calls.
- Activity report: linked artifact on the site; future project (Allo-IA) will automate documentation capture.

**AI interaction philosophy:** The visitor isn't here to give Syntonie data. They're here to feel seen. No interrogation — observation and resonance only.

### Top Concepts

#### Concept A: CREDENTIAL LAYER ARCHITECTURE
*Ideas 7, 2, 13, 17, 21*

A persistent, scannable trust surface that makes 13 years of institutional evidence immediately legible to anyone with a checklist. Elements: persistent credential bar at page top (founded, members, EU activities), pre-filled-form framing for key data, live clock or duration signal, proof stack sidebar. **The principle:** assessors are mentally filling forms — make it effortless by displaying Syntonie's data in the shape of the answers they're looking for.

**Why it matters:** The credibility gap isn't about lack of evidence. It's about evidence being buried in prose. This concept surfaces it structurally.

---

#### Concept B: THREE-DOOR HOMEPAGE
*Ideas 1, 15, 4*

Replace the undifferentiated homepage with an explicit audience routing layer. Three paths, three narrative arcs: *"Evaluating us for a grant? Start here."* / *"You're a partner institution? Start here."* / *"You have a project? Start here."* Each path is a different scroll experience with different voice, language depth, and calls to action. The homepage itself becomes a navigation act.

**Why it matters:** Three jobs, one website — serving all three from a single undifferentiated surface guarantees underserving all three. Routing is the design.

---

#### Concept C: THE SHELTER SECTION AS INVITATION
*Ideas 5, 10, 19, 23*

A dedicated page section — not a drawer, not a footnote — that presents the governance shelter model as an explicit offering. Components: the floor plan of ownership (what's shared vs. what's yours), 4 named precedents with object-label format (Speculoos, Fabulous, Cabinet de l'Étrange, Enigmaker), the invitation letter, and a "one honest failure" note. This converts a serendipitous word-of-mouth discovery into a structured, findable offering.

**Why it matters:** The project leader path currently only works through human accident. Making it explicit creates a new acquisition channel and reduces the "fear of absorption" barrier by naming it directly.

---

#### Concept D: CONSTELLATION AS EVIDENCE
*Ideas 20, 6, 12, 9*

Lead with what Syntonie enabled, not with what Syntonie is. The visual entry point is a constellation or portfolio grid: 4+ projects, each a named object with date, outcome, and brief. The architecture firm move: show the buildings first, explain the firm after. The constellation map makes the ecosystem legible at a glance and positions Syntonie as the invisible infrastructure that made everything else possible.

**Why it matters:** "We enabled this" is a stronger credibility claim than "we are structured as follows." Outcomes speak louder than governance descriptions — especially to assessors, who need to trace the line from association to project to beneficiary.

## 🛠️ PROTOTYPE: Make Ideas Tangible

### Prototype Approach

**The Fog-of-War Page**

The page starts almost empty — minimal, sparse, nearly silent. Not because there's nothing to say, but because the right content for *this visitor* hasn't been revealed yet. Interaction is the key. The more you engage, the more the page becomes yours.

This mirrors Syntonie itself: invisible by design, revealed through engagement.

**Prototype type:** High-fidelity interactive mockup in Pencil (.pen), exported as static HTML with vanilla JS interaction layer. AI calls proxied through a Cloudflare Worker (free tier, stateless).

**What we're testing:** Does the fog-of-war interaction model feel like discovery or confusion? Does the tuning fork input feel inviting or intimidating? Do the value toggles feel like recognition or a quiz?

---

### Prototype Description

#### Layer 0: The Initial State (the fog)

The page loads with almost nothing visible:
- Syntonie wordmark (top left)
- The tuning fork input (center, early in the page)
- A faint grid — the 59px Syntonie blueprint
- Nothing else. No nav, no hero text, no sections.

The emptiness is intentional. It creates mild cognitive dissonance: *where am I, what is this?* — which triggers curiosity in the exact visitor type we're designing for.

---

#### Layer 1: The Curiosity Trap (Concept 1)

**Component:** A single text input, styled like a search bar. No label. Placeholder text:
> *"What brought you here?"*
> *(or: "Describe what you're looking for — one sentence")*

**Mechanic:**
- As the visitor types, **autocomplete suggestions** appear — drawn from a pre-built array of Syntonie's topics:
  `neurodiversity, fablab, legal structure, governance, making, Belgium, Ixelles, youth, game design, civic tech, democracy, open source, AI documentation, maker faire, incubation, ASBL, Erasmus+, neuro-inclusion, repair, tools, creativity, collective, shelter...`
- Suggestions cascade: typing "neuro" surfaces `neurodiversity, neuro-inclusion, Allo-IA`
- Selecting or completing an entry triggers the **tuning fork**: the page begins to reveal content weighted toward that semantic cluster
- No submit button. No Enter required. Selection *is* the action.

**What happens after:** The fog lifts partially. A short paragraph appears — generated by Claude via JS → Cloudflare Worker → Claude API — contextually aware of Syntonie's actual offering and the selected topic. One paragraph. Not a chat. A resonance note.

*Example:* Visitor types "neurodiversity" → page surfaces the Allo-IA project card, the "Imparfait" value story, and the neuro-inclusive design thread. The rest remains in fog.

**Technical:** `topics.json` array in Hugo `data/` folder. Autocomplete in vanilla JS. Claude call is stateless: system prompt = Syntonie's full context; user message = the typed phrase; response = one paragraph max.

---

#### Layer 2: Values as Toggles with Stories (Concepts 4 + 7)

**Component:** Six value statements, arranged typographically — not as a list, as a composition. Initially: labels only, minimal.

```
Indépendant    Innovant    Belge
Imparfait    Prospectif    Open source
```

**Mechanic:**
- Each value is a **toggle**. Tapping/clicking unfolds a story panel beneath it.
- Stories are **human-written**, not AI-generated. Drawn from real Syntonie history:
  - *Indépendant* → "No operating subsidy since founding. Self-sustaining by design."
  - *Imparfait* → A named project that didn't scale, what was learned, why it mattered anyway.
  - *Open source* → The Enigmaker story: created at OpenFab, toured Europe, open-licensed.
  - *Prospectif* → Invisible Play and Allo-IA as two bets on where inclusive design is going.
  - *Innovant* → The governance shelter model: a legal innovation, not a tech one.
  - *Belge* → Why being Belgian, in Ixelles, with Commune support, is a feature not a limitation.
- Clicking a value also **signals the tuning fork** — combining with Layer 1 to further personalize what's visible.
- Values that resonate with the visitor's Layer 1 input **pulse subtly** — a soft visual cue that they're already in the right place.

**State:** Values start closed. Page remembers which are open (session-only, no persistence). Opened values contribute to the fog-lifting calculation.

---

#### Layer 3: The Slow Reveal (Concept 5)

**Trigger:** After 90+ seconds of genuine engagement (scroll depth + at least one toggle opened or Layer 1 interaction), a small, unobtrusive line appears at the bottom of the viewport — not a popup, not a modal:

> *"Vous lisez depuis un moment. Quelque chose vous a accroché·e ?"*
> *(or EN: "You've been reading a while. Something caught your attention?")*

A single text field below. No submit button — Enter sends.

**Claude responds once.** One sentence or two. Not task-completion. Not helpful in an onboarding sense. Helpful in a *we-heard-you* sense. Warm, direct, human in register.

*Example exchange:*
> Visitor: "I'm a designer who keeps getting asked to do things no one will pay for"
> Syntonie: "That's a familiar tension. Some of the projects in our constellation started exactly there."

The response never sells. It never asks a follow-up. It just reflects.

#### Layer 4: The Human Step (last, always last)

After all interaction — or after a direct scroll to the bottom — the final element:

**Not a form. Not a CTA button.** A short paragraph in first person:

> *"Si quelque chose ici résonne avec ce que vous construisez, la prochaine étape est humaine. Venez nous voir à OpenFab, ou écrivez-nous un message — pas un formulaire, juste un message."*

A single mailto link or a plain email address. That's it.

**Optional:** A linked PDF of the most recent activity report — for those who want to go deep before making contact. Labeled honestly: *"Rapport d'activités 2023 — on vous prévient, c'est détaillé."*

### Key Features to Test

**1. Does the empty initial state intrigue or alienate?**
The fog-of-war depends entirely on the visitor's willingness to engage with almost nothing. For a neurodivergent maker with high stimulation needs and strong self-direction — the hypothesis is that emptiness + a single input field is *more* inviting than a dense homepage. This is the riskiest assumption. Test: does the visitor type something within 15 seconds, or do they leave?

**2. Does the autocomplete feel like discovery or like being categorized?**
The topic array must feel like vocabulary the visitor would have chosen themselves — not a dropdown of Syntonie's internal categories. Test: do visitors complete suggestions, or do they ignore them and type freely?

**3. Do the value toggles feel like recognition or a quiz?**
The values must land as *honest self-description of Syntonie*, not as a test of whether the visitor shares those values. Test: do visitors open multiple values? Which ones get opened most? Does "Imparfait" resonate (honesty signal) or confuse?

**4. Does the slow reveal feel like a thoughtful moment or a surveillance signal?**
Appearing after 90 seconds of reading could feel warm (you've been paying attention) or creepy (the page is watching me). Test: do visitors engage with the whisper field, or do they close/ignore it?

**5. Does the Claude response in Layer 1 feel like recognition or like a chatbot?**
One paragraph from Claude, no follow-up. The test is whether the tone lands as human/warm or as AI-generic. This is entirely dependent on the system prompt quality and response constraints. Test: do visitors continue reading after the Layer 1 response, or does the interaction feel concluded?

**6. Does "write us a message — not a form" feel accessible to the target visitor?**
For someone who doesn't fill cold forms, a plain email address can be either liberating (no structure required) or paralyzing (now I have to write something). Test: what's the conversion rate from page visit to email sent? And does the contact email read like a person or like an info@ address?

## ✅ TEST: Validate with Users

### Testing Plan

**Validated assumption (pre-test):**
Emptiness reduces cognitive load for the target visitor — neurodivergent makers with high stimulation sensitivity. The fog-of-war initial state is a *feature*, not a bug. This assumption is confirmed by the primary persona (Nicolas) and does not need further testing. It should be held as a design constraint, not a variable.

**Remaining assumptions to test (in priority order):**

---

**Test 1 — The Curiosity Trap activation rate**
*Does the visitor type something within 15–20 seconds of landing?*

Who: 3–5 people from the OpenFab/Politype ecosystem who haven't seen the site before. Not random users — specifically: makers with a project idea, people who've heard of Syntonie vaguely, people who self-identify as non-standard thinkers.

Method: Unmoderated screen recording (Loom or equivalent). Observer watches without intervening. Task: "Land on this page. Do whatever you would normally do."

What to observe:
- Do they type in the field within 20 seconds?
- Do they use the autocomplete, or type freely past it?
- Do they read the Claude response, or ignore it?
- What did they type? (log for topics array calibration)

Success signal: 4/5 visitors engage with the field within 20 seconds.
Failure signal: Visitors scroll past the field looking for "real content."

---

**Test 2 — Value toggle resonance**
*Do the values land as honest self-description, not as a test?*

Same visitors. After Test 1 observation. Ask: "What did you make of those words?" (pointing to the six values). Then: "Did you open any of them? Why that one?"

What to observe:
- Which values get opened? "Imparfait" is the hypothesis leader (honesty signal).
- Do visitors open one and stop, or cascade through several?
- Does any value feel confusing or off-brand?
- Do they read the full story, or scan and close?

Success signal: Visitors open at least 2 values and can articulate why one resonated.
Failure signal: "I didn't realize I could click those."

---

**Test 3 — The slow reveal tone**
*Does appearing after 90 seconds feel warm or surveillance-adjacent?*

This is the most delicate test. Don't trigger it artificially — let it emerge naturally during the session. After it appears, ask: "What did you think when that appeared?"

What to observe:
- First reaction: curiosity, warmth, irritation, confusion?
- Do they type something, or ignore it?
- If they type — is it earnest (a real thought) or performative (testing the system)?

Success signal: Visitor types something genuine. Describes the moment as "nice" or "unexpected in a good way."
Failure signal: "Oh, another one of those." / "It felt like the site was watching me."

If failure: adjust trigger to require more explicit engagement (e.g., at least 2 values opened + Layer 1 used) before the slow reveal fires.

---

**Test 4 — The human step friction**
*Does "write us a message — not a form" convert, or paralyze?*

Not testable in a single session — requires a real deployment period. But can be partially tested by asking at end of session: "If you wanted to reach out after visiting this page, what would you do?"

What to observe:
- Do they mention the email address unprompted?
- Do they describe what they would write — or do they say "I wouldn't know what to say"?
- Does the activity report PDF feel like a door-opener or a homework assignment?

Success signal: Visitor says "I'd just write something casual" or "I'd send a short note about [x]."
Failure signal: "I wouldn't know how to start" → needs a prompt or suggested subject line.

---

**Test 5 — Claude response tone (Layer 1)**
*Does the one-paragraph response feel like recognition or like a chatbot?*

After the session, show visitors the Claude response they received (or show a mock response if the API wasn't live). Ask: "Does this feel like it was written by a person who knows Syntonie, or by a machine?"

What to observe:
- Can they tell it's AI? (It's fine if they can — the question is whether it feels *warm* or *generic*)
- Does it make them want to keep reading, or feel like the interaction is over?
- Does the response accurately reflect Syntonie's voice and offering?

Success signal: "It felt like someone actually read what I wrote."
Failure signal: "It's clearly AI and it said nothing specific."

If failure: tighten the system prompt. Add more Syntonie-specific context, named projects, specific language from the charter. Constrain response length to 2 sentences max.

**Feedback capture method:**
After each session: one unstructured question — "What surprised you about this page?" — before any directed debrief. The surprise answer often reveals the most important signal.
